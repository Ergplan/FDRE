# FDRE Optimizer — Model Explanation Notes

Technical notes on how the engine (`fdre_enterprise_engine.py`) models the NHPC FDRE Tranche-II tender. Intended for analysts validating results and developers extending the code.

## 1. What the tender asks for (as encoded)

NHPC T-II procures firm & dispatchable power in MW terms. The RPD declares an annual CUF (≥ 40%) and must (a) keep the contracted capacity at least 90% available during four daily peak hours — two in 05:00–10:00, two in 18:00–23:00, buyer-scheduled — tested monthly, and (b) deliver annual energy within −15%/+10% of the declared CUF, with NHPC's offtake capped at +10%. Liquidated damages are 1.5 × tariff on shortfall units for both tests, and both can apply in the same year. Up to 5% of annual energy may be sourced from green markets. Energy beyond the contracted capacity (or offered but unscheduled) may be sold to third parties/exchanges. The tariff payment nets a ₹0.02/kWh PSM charge. Securities: EMD ₹9.28/12.64/14.64 L per MW (solar/wind/ESS) capped at ₹10 cr per project; PBG ₹23.20/31.60/36.60 L per MW; success charge ₹1 L/MW + GST; slab processing fee.

## 2. Resource profiles

Synthetic 8,760-hour capacity-factor traces per generator, calibrated so the annual mean equals the input CUF (iterative scale-and-clip at 1.0). Solar: cosine-bell diurnal shape with month-specific day length, monthly seasonality with monsoon depression, lognormal-ish day-to-day noise (wider in monsoon). Wind: monsoon-heavy monthly shape, mild afternoon/evening diurnal tilt, persistent AR(1) hourly noise. Each generator gets a stable seed offset, so portfolios are reproducible for a given weather seed. Custom measured traces can be supplied per generator through the model layer (`gen_<name>` / `<name>_cf` / `<technology>_cf` columns).

Degradation enters as a per-year multiplier on each generator's trace ((1 − d)^(year − 1)). BESS ageing is separate: a state-of-health curve scales usable MWh by year (the bundled curve is taken from the uploaded BESS spec, ~4.3% fade in year 2 tapering to ~1.5%/yr, resetting to 1.0 at the year-11 augmentation), and an RTE curve steps down with age. Beyond the curve's last point the final value holds.

## 3. Dispatch algorithm

For each simulated year the engine builds the hourly peak-hour target (contracted MW in the four peak hours) and walks all 8,760 hours chronologically. Within each **peak hour**: renewable generation at each grid node supplies the target first (node interconnection limits respected, pro-rata across nodes when RE exceeds the target); any remaining deficit is met by discharging batteries in descending-SOC order, each constrained by its power rating, SOC and its node's remaining headroom; surplus RE then charges local batteries (same node only — this is what guarantees RE-only charging); whatever is left is sold merchant up to node headroom, with the configured curtailment haircut, and the remainder spills. Within each **non-peak hour**: batteries charge first from local RE; remaining exportable energy is scheduled to the PPA up to the contracted capacity and up to the annual cap of 110% × declared CUF (a reservation mechanism holds back enough cap headroom for the remaining peak hours of the year); the rest goes merchant.

SOC carries over hour to hour and day to day. Because the year is circular, the engine iterates the year several times (default 3 + 1 reporting pass), feeding the ending SOC back as the starting SOC until converged.

Monthly compliance: peak availability = delivered peak energy (plus any green support) ÷ peak-hour obligation; shortfall is measured against the 90% floor. Annual compliance: PPA energy vs declared CUF − 15% on the 8,766-hour convention. If green support is enabled, the 5% allowance is allocated to monthly-peak shortfalls first (both damages can apply, and peak damage is otherwise unavoidable), then to the annual test. Penalty energy is priced at 1.5 × tariff in the financial model — but by default "hard compliance" refuses to solve a tariff for any configuration with penalty energy at all.

**Solve modes.** Fast mode dispatches years 1, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and linearly interpolates all annual aggregates in between (validated to ~0.2% on required tariff for the bundled portfolio); Exact mode dispatches every year. Because compliance in skipped years is interpolated, always confirm final numbers in Exact mode.

## 4. Financial model

Capex = per-asset EPC (solar ₹cr/MWac, wind ₹cr/MW, BESS split into energy ₹cr/MWh + PCS ₹cr/MW) + land/development + transmission lump sum + owner's costs + success charge + processing fee, plus contingency, giving hard cost; IDC accrues monthly on debt drawn per the construction drawdown profile over 24 months. Debt = debt fraction × (hard + IDC); annuity repayment over the tenor.

Revenue = PPA energy × (tariff − ₹0.02 PSM) + merchant energy × merchant price (escalating) − penalties (1.5 × tariff × shortfall MWh) − green support purchase cost. Opex = per-asset O&M (escalating) + admin + insurance on hard capex + working-capital interest on receivables. Tax follows Sec 115BAA: 25.168% on income after 40% WDV depreciation on the eligible block (95% of project cost; augmentation added to the block in its year) plus straight-line on the balance, with unabsorbed losses carried forward. CFADS = EBITDA − tax − ΔWC; FCFE = CFADS − debt service − augmentation capex. Book depreciation (SLM to 5% residual) feeds PAT only.

Equity IRR is computed on construction-phased equity outflows followed by FCFE (first outflow at t = 0 in the discounting convention); project IRR on total cost vs CFADS − augmentation. DSCR = CFADS ÷ debt service (a post-augmentation DSCR series is also reported). **Caution:** the year-11 full augmentation creates a mid-term negative equity cash flow (3 sign changes), so the IRR root is not guaranteed unique; the reported root satisfies NPV = 0 and NPV at the discount rate is reported alongside — prefer NPV when ranking options with different augmentation timing.

**Required tariff** is solved by bisection: the lowest tariff at which equity IRR ≥ target *and* min DSCR ≥ floor. Hard-compliance failures return NaN with the failing reason rather than a price.

## 5. Capacity optimizer

Search variables: wind MW, solar MWac, BESS power MW, BESS energy MWh, contracted capacity MW (rounded to 10 MW multiples). The candidate set is seeded with the current configuration, the bounds midpoint, a **HiGHS LP seed** (minimum proxy-capex point satisfying linearised annual-energy and peak-power constraints, plus duration variants), a cost-aware **bid-scaled mix grid** (solar 1.0–2.5x and wind 0–1.0x of each candidate bid size, with storage bracketing the 90% peak-power floor up to 1.25x at peak-window-scaled durations), a compliance-repair push per bid size, three fractional points, and bounded random samples (the random exploration always runs, independent of the grid size). Each candidate is scored by required tariff (plus small capex and spill tie-breakers) through the full dispatch + finance pipeline. The winner is then refined by a **greedy coordinate descent** that always runs — including Fast effort — walking each variable in each direction while the score keeps improving (1 round at Fast, up to 3 rounds with halved steps at Balanced/Thorough). The app then re-solves the winning configuration over the exact 25-year term. Results are screening-grade local minima, not certified global optima.

## 6. Conventions and known limitations

Operating year is 8,760 hours while CUF obligations use the RfS 8,766-hour convention (≈0.07% conservative). Availability compliance is evaluated in energy terms at the delivery point (the RfS applies the penalty to "shortfall in generation corresponding to the shortfall in availability"). The buyer is assumed to schedule the full contracted capacity in every non-peak hour (configurable take fraction) — this drives the annual CUF outcome and is an offtake assumption, not a tender guarantee. Merchant sales use a single escalating price with a haircut, no hourly market shape. Fixed peak hours by default; the worst-deficit mode is a stress case (the bundled portfolio sits exactly at 90.0% year-1 availability under it). The bundled SOH/RTE curves and the year-11 augmentation are a matched pair — the `validate()` warning added in QA flags any mismatch between BESS-spec augmentation years and the finance schedule. Everything is screening-grade; replace synthetic profiles, cost and tax assumptions with project data before investment decisions.
